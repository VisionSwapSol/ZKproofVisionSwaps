6. Private sends, buys and sells

6.1 How they work. Your browser builds a zero-knowledge proof that the payment is yours to make. A relayer, normally ours, submits it to Solana and pays the network fee, which is repaid from the payment. The recipient, the amount, the relayer fee and the highest pool fee you accept are bound into the proof, so the relayer can't change them.

6.2 This website needs our servers. The website relies on our servers for relaying, screening and chain data. The Pool program doesn't depend on them, but we don't promise any other way to use it.

6.3 Refusals and delays. Our relayer can turn down, hold back or queue a payment, for instance when it is rate-limited or busy, when fees change, after a screening result, or for legal or security reasons.

6.4 Private sends. A private send pays SOL out of the Pool to the address you enter. A send to an address outside your own VisionSwap wallets goes through a one-time sending address made for that payment, which only your app holds the key to, and reaches the recipient as a standard SOL transfer from it. Anyone can see that address, the amount, the fees and when it happened. The recipient is screened too, and we may decline to pay it.

6.5 Buys and sells. A private buy moves SOL from the Pool to one of your Vision wallets, which then trades through Jupiter, a third-party aggregator that routes through other exchanges. Prices can move before a trade completes, within the slippage you accept, and trades can fail. Before you confirm, the app shows the minimum you will receive, and the trade is built to fail rather than deliver less. A faster speed setting adds a network priority fee, up to the cap the app shows. A sale swaps back to SOL and deposits it into the Pool, which pays the deposit fee; a sale too small for a deposit leaves its SOL in the Vision wallet. Vision wallets, what they hold and everything they do, including being funded from the Pool, are public on-chain; VisionSwap only avoids funding them from your own wallet.

6.6 Tokens. We don't create, review, list or endorse tokens: the app can show any token Jupiter can trade. Many tokens are worthless or fraudulent, and some let their issuer freeze, tax or block transfers. You buy any token at your own risk.

6.7 Check before you confirm. Double-check every address, token and amount before you confirm. Anything sent to the wrong address is gone for good.

6.8 Sending later, and several payments at once. If you choose to send later, your browser makes the proof straight away and our relayer submits it at a random moment within the window you chose. When the Pool is busy, it may submit the payment earlier than planned, still within that window, so that its proof does not expire, and while the app is open it may make a fresh proof shortly before the payment goes out. The relayer can only send that exact payment, or not send it. Until it goes out, the notes it spends are set aside and VisionSwap won't spend them elsewhere; you can cancel it until then. If they are spent another way first, the scheduled payment fails and nothing is sent. If it isn't sent, those funds remain yours. A payment that still needs a fresh proof (for example when the Pool has changed a lot) waits until the app makes one, the next time you open it. While our relayer is being topped up, scheduled payments wait. To hold back only what it sends, a later payment usually first sets aside a note of its own size within your private balance, which costs a relayer fee. Sending to several recipients makes one payment each, each with its own fees; several payments sent "now" go out one by one at random moments over about two minutes. Every private payment to another wallet or an exchange arrives as a standard SOL transfer from a one-time sending address, so exchanges and wallets credit it like any other deposit.

6.9 Sending it yourself. If our relayer is unavailable, you can submit a private send with a fee payer of your own: one of your account's fee wallets (by default a new one for each payment) or a Vision wallet. That fee payer is visible in the transaction and pays the network costs up front; the payment repays them. This way sends one payment at a time. Every private payment to another wallet or an exchange arrives as a standard SOL transfer from a one-time sending address, so exchanges and wallets credit it like any other deposit.

6.10 Payment links. A payment link points to a fresh deposit address of your account, and is meant for one payer. What is paid to it is screened and moves into your private balance like any deposit, with the same fees and rules, when your account is open in the app; several paid links move in one at a time, a few minutes apart. The payer and the amount they send to that address are visible on-chain.

6.11 Orders. Limit buys, DCA, take-profit, stop-loss and trailing-stop orders are run by our order service. Each order has its own key, derived from your account. When you place one, the Vision wallet holding the tokens or SOL gives that key on-chain permission to move up to exactly the amount the order sets aside; the token program enforces that amount, and our order service sends the proceeds back to the same Vision wallet. While the order is live, that key, which our service holds, can move those tokens or that SOL; nothing else in the wallet or the Pool. For a DCA, that is its whole budget, for as long as the DCA runs. Our service keeps order details encrypted when stored and deletes an order's key from its records when the order ends. Cancelling the order revokes that permission on-chain, with a network fee paid from the Vision wallet. If an order ends another way, the app removes any permission left on-chain when it is next open on the device that placed the order, or offers you a way to remove it.

6.12 How orders run. Orders watch market prices from Jupiter and trade through Jupiter when the price you set is reached, within the price room you chose. A limit buy or a take-profit step trades only if the trade's own quote meets your price, and the least it must deliver is set in the transaction, so, measured with Jupiter's prices at the time of the trade, it doesn't fill at a worse price than yours (on a sale, a token's own transfer tax comes on top); when the market can't meet it, it keeps waiting. A stop-loss normally sells only after a second, newer price confirms the first; if the token has no market price for a few minutes, a live sell quote at or below the stop can trigger it. It then sells at the market within its own price room, so it can sell below its price; it does not sell if the price has clearly recovered above the stop. A trailing stop follows the highest price seen since the order started or was last changed. DCA buys are made at the market, and unless you choose exact timing each one is moved by a random amount of up to 10% of the interval, at most 30 minutes. Trades wait their turn in a queue that puts stop-losses first. Changes you make to a running order apply from the next price check without a new approval; an order's amount can only be lowered, and the on-chain permission stays at the amount first set aside until the order ends or is cancelled. Our relayer pays the network fees of order trades. The proceeds of a sale stay in the Vision wallet until the app moves them into the Pool, the next time it is open. We don't guarantee that an order fills, fills at any particular time, or fills at all: prices can move fast, routes can be missing, and our service or Jupiter can be unavailable or delayed. Tokens that tax transfers pay that tax one extra time when an order runs. Orders end when they fill, expire or are cancelled, or when the tokens or SOL leave the Vision wallet, and we may cancel an order at any time. You remain responsible for your orders and their results.

6.13 Paying from your wallet. When you pay from your connected wallet on the Send tab, your wallet makes one deposit into the Pool of a standard size that covers the payment and its fees, and the deposit goes through screening and the waiting room like any other. What the payment doesn't use stays in your private balance as change. The payment's details (the recipient, the amount, the timing you chose, and which deposit pays for it) are kept on that device only, and are never sent to our servers ahead of the payment. Once the deposit joins your private balance, the app proves the payment and hands it to our relayer automatically, sent at once or at a random moment within the window you chose. The app has to be open, or opened again on the same device, for that to happen; until it does, the SOL stays in your private balance. On another device it shows as private balance, not as a payment. You can stop the payment until it is handed over, and the SOL then stays in your private balance, or take the deposit back to your wallet while it is in the waiting room. A payment more than a day old, or one whose fees rose above what its deposit covers, waits for you to decide. If the payment can't be made (for example because its recipient is refused), nothing is sent and the SOL stays in your private balance.

6.14 Actions across several Vision wallets. When you buy, sell, move back or send with several Vision wallets at once, the app in your browser runs each wallet's action in turn, with the gap you chose, as if you had started each one yourself. The list of wallets and where each one got to are kept on that device only. Keep the page open while it runs: if it closes, the wallets that haven't had their turn keep what they hold, and the app offers to continue or stop the next time it is open on the same device, after checking where an interrupted wallet got to. Each wallet's action can fail on its own (for example when a route is missing or the price moves past your price room); the others still run. Spacing the wallets out and varying their amounts makes them harder to link to each other, not impossible.
